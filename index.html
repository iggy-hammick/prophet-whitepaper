    The readiness scale

    From stretched to poised

    Success in 2027 hinges around finding a balance between three pivotal tensions. For each one, we rate how well prepared the average team is on a three-point readiness scale.

    1. Stretched

      Teams are pulled hard to one side. The biggest gaps, in need of active attention.

    2. Precarious

      Teams lean to one side and could tip if the imbalance isn’t managed.

    3. Poised

      Teams are broadly balanced, but still need focus to stay that way.

    What the data shows

    Our data shows that, in their eagerness to embrace efficiency and technological advancement, businesses may miss the urgent need to focus on people. Across our dataset, teams are almost exactly balanced between people and things, whether financial figures, buildings, stock levels or research data.

    The significance of this lean is exacerbated by the sheer speed of the AI revolution. Is anyone taking the time to think about what is lost before it’s too late?

    Exco n = 223 · Other leadership n = 511 · Teams n = 1,267 · scale ±14

    Recommendations

    In order to succeed in 2027 and beyond, organisations may need to dial up their focus on people and relationships. It will be vital to put training into place, as well as intentionally driving relationships and mutual trust in all directions; up, down, sideways and diagonally across the business. Successful teams will still be able to problem-solve, innovate and improve together.

    Leading organisations are already intentionally rebuilding the human learning curve, context-sharing and coaching channels that automated systems bypass.

    Tension 2 of 3

    StabilityversusAgility

    Rated Precarious: true forward thinking includes the present, yet appetite for change is outrunning the capacity to embed it.

    Still until the ground shakes.

    Jump, land, steady again.

    The operating environment of 2027 demands an unprecedented blend of structural stability and rapid adaptability – a condition that some have termed “stagility.” Teams engineered to operate at 100% bandwidth inevitably creak when faced with unexpected disruptions, potentially leading to operational delays and widespread burnout.

    PROPHET data reflects that teams skew towards the excitement of the future. The rate of change and scale of opportunity is intoxicating, and no one wants to be left behind. However, to realise visionary plans, day-to-day operations must stay grounded, capital must be secured for growth and reputation must be protected.

    “Who is managing the disconnect between day-to-day operations and future strategy?”

    What the data shows

    Perhaps unsurprisingly, the numbers indicate that teams skew towards the excitement of the future. The rate of change and scale of opportunity is intoxicating. No one wants to be left behind. Leaders in particular lean strongly towards the promise of what’s to come.

    However, to realise visionary plans, day-to-day operations must stay grounded, capital must be secured for growth and reputation must be protected.

    Exco n = 223 · Other leadership n = 511 · Teams n = 1,267 · scale ±14

    Recommendations

    To successfully navigate the dynamics of “stagility” and relieve the pressure on the middle layer, each tier of the organisation would benefit from adapting its focus. True forward thinking includes a focus on the present.

    Organisations should plan how to manage the disconnect between the present and the future, as well as making clear plans for change management that bring the whole team along for the ride.

    Tension 3 of 3

    ProximityversusEngagement

    Rated Stretched: as teams shapeshift from hierarchy to network, they demand both flexibility and structure.

    Close together, others alone.

    Reaching out, pulling in.

    Hybrid and distributed working arrangements are no longer treated as novel perks or temporary compromises: in 2027, they represent a settled baseline of professional work. However, most organisations have not yet tackled the operational complexity they create.

    Hybrid, flexible teams need much more Managing to empower them to work together effectively. Yet PROPHET’s evidence reveals a strong skew towards Leading. To maintain alignment and enable recognition, hybrid and distributed teams need transparent, relationship-led workflows, deep understanding of context and the right support at the right moment.

    “How can organisations proactively build an equitable, high-performing, hybrid culture?”

    What the data shows

    PROPHET’s evidence reveals a strong skew towards Leading, leaving a wide managing gap to fill as teams continue to shapeshift, stretching across physical locations and timezones.

    The reality of hybrid, remote, flexible teams is that they need much more managing to empower them to work together effectively. Whether building the right environment for innovative brainstorming or conscientious problem-solving, or establishing the psychological safety required to admit fault, the right structures and processes are crucial.

    Without deliberate structure, remote setups can inadvertently create two-tiered team dynamics, where access to context and career advancement favours those in physical proximity to senior leadership.

    Exco n = 223 · Other leadership n = 511 · Teams n = 1,267 · scale ±14

    Recommendations

    To build an equitable, high-performing hybrid culture, each tier of the organisation should adapt its habits, communication methods and leadership style.

    Team leaders in particular may need to lean into managing. In simple terms, this means listening and supporting your teams through change. Key skills will include coaching, learning how to spot the signs of burnout early and ensuring you have visibility of your people’s workload.

    To maintain alignment, hybrid and distributed teams need transparent, relationship-led workflows, deep understanding of context and the right support at the right moment. Effective leadership ensures teams engage on specific outputs and deliverables, no matter the physical or digital divides.

    The sector breakdown

    Using PROPHET benchmark data, we can also evaluate how well-equipped different industry sectors are to handle the demands of 2027.

    2027 continues to bring change and challenge at an exhausting pace. The good news is that, across the PROPHET Team Dynamics Index, the average team leans towards Leading over Managing, the Future over the Present, and Flexibility over Structure. These are teams that want to set direction, embrace change and move quickly. In 12 of the 14 sectors, teams are strongest in the Roles linked to the Create phase of the business cycle: sparking ideas, setting a bold direction and taking commercial risks.

    Recommendations · five priority actions

    Five priority actions for 2027

    1. 1 · Put managing back on the board agenda. Treat coaching, fair visibility and workload as leadership metrics.
    2. 2 · Pair visionaries with operators. Give every major change an Operate-minded owner alongside its sponsor.
    3. 3 · Equip the middle to be the bridge. Train leaders to turn direction into process and teach it.
    4. 4 · Protect the teams carrying the load. Build capacity buffers so the most balanced level isn't the most overloaded.
    5. 5 · Rebuild the channels that AI bypasses. Design coaching and context-sharing into AI-led workflows.
